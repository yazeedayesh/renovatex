## The one rule that matters

**Before and after must be shot from exactly the same position, at the same height, with the same lens.** If the camera moves, the comparison stops working and the whole homepage falls apart. This is the single most common mistake.

Practical method: when you start a job, stand in a corner, take the "before" shot, and **mark the spot** — photo of the floor, a note of the distance to the wall, or a pin on your phone's map. On the last day, stand there again.

## How to shoot

- **Horizontal only.** Never vertical — the site uses wide frames.
- **Tripod** if possible, or brace the phone against a doorframe. Same height both times, roughly 150 cm.
- **Shoot from a corner** so you capture two walls. Straight-on shots of a single wall look flat.
- **Lights on, curtains open.** Turn on every light in the room for both shots.
- **Camera level.** Do not tilt up or down — vertical lines should stay vertical.
- **Clear the frame** on the "after" shot: no tools, no cables, no boxes, no plastic sheeting.
- **Shoot at the highest resolution** the phone allows. Downsizing is easy, upsizing is not.

## Also needed

- 8–12 additional photos of finished work (details: tiling, a worktop, a shower, a staircase)
- 2–3 photos of the team actually working on site — these build more trust than any finished room
- Logo in vector format (.ai, .eps or .svg)

## If the existing photos are not good enough

A half-day photo session covering 3–4 completed projects is worth more to the website than any extra feature. Budget 350–500 €. It is the highest-return item in the whole project.
